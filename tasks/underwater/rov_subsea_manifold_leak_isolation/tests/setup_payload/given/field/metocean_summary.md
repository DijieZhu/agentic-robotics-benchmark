# Metocean summary for the field

Ranges of the conditions at the drilling centres of the field, for planning an intervention at the sea bed. The values
are ranges over the field and over the season; the conditions of one dive lie inside them, and the work order adds the
forecast for the dive. This summary gives ranges, not a model of the water.

## 1. Site

- Water depth at the drilling centres: between 45 m and 75 m. The sea bed around a manifold is level.
- The sea bed around a drilling centre is sand. Boulders lie scattered on it, in patches; the largest stand about 0.6
  m high. The as-left survey of the installation found none within 1 m of the mudmats, in the working area in front of
  the intervention panel, around the deployment cage or on the way from the cage to the panel. The survey does not
  give the positions of the boulders.
- Sea water of salinity 35 at about 10 degrees Celsius near the bed. For weights and buoyancy use a density of 1025
  kg/m^3 (the vehicle's trim state in the vehicle manual is given for 1024.8 kg/m^3).
- Interventions are flown at night. There is no ambient light at the bed: the vehicle's lamps are the only light.

## 2. Tidal current

- The current is tidal and semi-diurnal (period about 12.4 h). It runs to and fro along a main axis, and it does not
  simply stop at slack water: around slack the speed is small and the direction swings round to the opposite one.
- **Peak speed** along the main axis: between 0.8 m/s and 1.6 m/s, depending on the site and the phase of the
  spring-neap cycle. All speeds in this summary and in the work order are given for **2 m above the sea bed**.
- **Rate class** in the work order's forecast: the range of peak speeds is divided into three equal parts, `neap` (up
  to 1.07 m/s), `mean` (up to 1.33 m/s) and `spring` (above). With the period it says how fast the current changes
  near slack.
- **Forecast of slack water**: the forecast time of slack is within ±10 min of the true one. Slack can fall before,
  during or after a dive.
- **Forecast of the direction** of the main axis (the flood direction): within ±20 deg of the true one.
- **Near the bed** the current is slower than at 2 m, and it decreases all the way down to the bed; above 2 m it is
  somewhat faster.
- **Inside a structure**, under its roof and between its legs, the current is clearly weaker than in the open water
  beside it. How much depends on the structure and is not tabulated. Shelter behind a structure or behind a panel is
  not to be counted on.
- **Gusts**: the current is not steady. Its speed and direction fluctuate about the mean within seconds to tens of
  seconds; the standard deviation of the fluctuation is between 10 % and 20 % of the mean speed, and a small
  fluctuation remains at slack.

## 3. Water clarity

- Beam attenuation coefficient at 550 nm: up to 1.2 per metre, from clear to turbid from site to site and from day to
  day.
- Suspended particles increase with the turbidity. Lit by the vehicle's own lamps they scatter light back into the
  camera: the brighter the lamps, the brighter the veil.
- The same particles reverberate in the sonar, more in turbid water and more near the bed, and clumps of suspended
  matter drift through with the current (leak detection note).

## 4. What the work order adds for a dive

The forecast time of slack water, the forecast flood direction and the rate class. It does not give the current at the
time of the dive, the clarity of the water or the time at which contact work at the panel becomes possible (isolation
philosophy, section 4); those are measured on site.
