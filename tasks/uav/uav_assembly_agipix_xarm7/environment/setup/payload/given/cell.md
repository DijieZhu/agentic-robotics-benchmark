# xArm 7 assembly cell

The bench surface is world z=0, approximately 1.6 by 1.5 metres. World x points from the arm base towards the fixture, and z points upward. The arm base is at approximately (-0.40, 0, 0) m; the lower chassis is clamped about 0.18 m above the bench near (0.08, 0). The square pedestal contacts the chassis centre and leaves the four underside landing-strut interfaces accessible. The ten alternating 20 mm squares near the far bench edge are a physical scale reference.

The arm and original parallel gripper use the supplied `given/xarm7/xarm7.xml` kinematic chain, inertia and actuator force limits. The workcell moves its root body to the commissioned base location. Joint targets and readouts are in radians, in `joint1` through `joint7` order. The gripper's +z axis points towards the fingertips; its jaws close along ±y. `link_tcp` is 172 mm from the gripper base; the two contact pads are nearer the base, so calculate grasp offsets using the supplied geometry. The target width is an inner-face opening in metres, between 0 and 0.085. The stock model's closed linkage stop leaves about 1.6 mm of measured gap even at a zero-width command; verify the reported `gripper_width_m` instead of assuming the requested opening was achieved. For a force-holding pinch, command slightly less than the measured object width. Grasping uses frictional contacts: there is no attach command.

The joint controller performs gravity compensation through the same bounded actuators, with position feedback and a small integral correction. It does not apply unbounded external forces. Robot and payload motion continue during every `step`, including after contact. Keep targets away from joint limits and allow tracking to settle before a precision insertion.

`get_state()` returns time, seven joint positions and velocities, the wrist force/torque vector (N followed by Nm, in the sensor frame), measured jaw opening, remaining camera frames and whether the workcell has finished. The wrench includes gravity/inertial loads, an unknown fixed bias and sensor noise. It is not a contact classifier.

The top camera is about 1.65 m above the middle of the bench. The wrist camera is on the gripper's +x flank and looks forward towards its fingers. Mounts and focal lengths are approximate and vary between workcells; infer calibration from geometry, known arm motion and visible scale cues. RGB is rendered by MuJoCo's Filament renderer (physically based: soft shadows, reflections on metal and plastic; on the CPU through Vulkan, Mesa's lavapipe) in the workshop hall of `render/`, whose light is baked into its textures. `given/lib/cell_render.py` is that renderer for your own twin of the cell, and `render.filament.view` renders any scene of yours the same way (`given/lib/README.md`); they are the way to see what these cameras would see. MuJoCo's own renderer (`mujoco.Renderer`, or the render layer's `"mujoco"` backend) draws a different picture (no workshop hall, MuJoCo's default lights, flat colours, no reflections), so image processing tuned on its frames may behave differently on the cameras' frames. Depth is optical-axis distance registered to RGB, quantized to millimetres, with a 0.1–4 m usable range and about 3% missing pixels. Zero means invalid. The wrist camera can therefore have no depth on very close objects during grasping.

Only the lower chassis is fixed. Other parts rest freely; the propellers and the thin panels without captive feet each lie on a small printed staging stand fixed to the bench (a base plate with posts; a propeller sits on a pedestal under its hub). The stands hold the part's underside 26 mm above the bench and leave finger access under it. The guards and compute cover include their captive spacer sets. The guard deck also carries two TPU sensor-cage cradles, and the camera unit includes its two source bushes and a CF camera bridge whose crossbar rests on the deck; all are captive hardware bundled with those components. All collider meshes are in `given/kit/collision/`; part metadata names every piece. Source plate profiles are simplified by at most 0.15 mm for contact and fastener bores below 25 mm² are filled. Large rotor openings and assembly clearances remain open. A smooth visual mesh alone is not the collision model.

Camera requests do not advance physics. `step(n)` advances n control ticks, unless a budget or safety limit ends the cell. `done` does not reveal assembly progress. Any call outside the documented interface is rejected.

The sustained part-load limit sums contact-force magnitudes across all collision pieces of a physical part, so dividing a surface into more convex pieces does not weaken the limit. Non-pad external contacts are likewise summed per robot body. Nonadjacent robot self-contact is monitored; mechanically adjacent joint housings and the bolted arm base are exempt from the collision alarm.
