# Build log of the reference unit

Kept by the lab. The entries BL-01 to BL-10 were written up from the bench notes of the colleague who took the
photographs, and each statement was checked against the car before it left.

**BL-01. The car.** The reference unit is a tenth-scale autonomous race car. The lab built it in March 2025
as a demonstration car for the lab's open house and its teaching; it is the only one of its kind. It left the lab on
2 November 2026 on a long loan and cannot be measured. The photographs PH-01 to PH-33 were taken on
the lab's bench in the week before it left.

**BL-02. The rolling chassis.** The car stands on the rolling chassis kit AVH-YTXE of the quote (data page
DS-01). The replicas are built on the same kit. The parts list of the kit gives the spur gear 61 teeth, the ring gear of the front differential 39 teeth and the bevel drive pinion of the front gearbox 15 teeth; this pinion drives the ring gear. The gear case encloses the bevel drive pinion, and no photograph shows it.

**BL-03. Weighing.** The car and the pod were weighed on the lab's kitchen scale. The scale reads in steps of
1 g and was set to zero, empty, before each weighing. The whole car was weighed with all four wheels on the
platform. For the front axle the two front wheels stood on the platform and the two rear wheels on a block as
high as the platform, so that the car stood level. The readings are on the display of the scale in the
photographs (BL-10); they were not written down anywhere else.

The steering servo is the servo that came with the rolling chassis kit. It carries no maker's name, weighs
37.2 g, and is not an item of the catalogue.

**BL-05. Steering lock.** The steering is set to a lock of 24.0 degrees: from straight ahead a
front wheel turns through this angle to full lock, to either side. In every photograph the wheels point straight
ahead.

**BL-06. How the pictures were archived.** The photographs are not the files of the camera. When the lab's
report archive took the pictures in, it cropped each one at its left and its right edge, stretched it in the
horizontal direction and sheared it in the horizontal direction, by amounts that differ from picture to picture,
and saved all of them at one common size. The camera's files, the amounts and the data of the camera were not
kept. The pixel axes of a picture are therefore not a calibrated pair of axes, and the format of a picture says
nothing about the camera or about how the picture was taken.

**BL-07. The deck and its posts.** The deck plate is carried by four deck posts of 48.2 mm, the item
HFH-QBWQ of BL-04. The posts pass through the deck plate, and their lower ends do not rest on the chassis
tub. The replicas are put together in the same way, whatever the length of their posts: a post that is longer by
some amount carries the deck plate, and everything on the deck plate, higher by the same amount.

**BL-08. Scale references.** These objects of known size stand in the photographs:

- PH-02: a drinks can, 66 mm across its body and 122 mm tall. It stands on the bench behind the tail of the car.
- PH-05: a steel rule graduated over 300 mm in steps of 1 mm and numbered in centimetres, its zero at the end of the rule. It lies flat on the bench behind the car, across the length of the car.
- PH-06: an ID-1 card, 85.60 mm by 53.98 mm. It lies flat on the bench by the nose of the car.
- PH-07: a drinks can, 66 mm across its body and 122 mm tall. It stands on the bench by the nose of the car.
- PH-09: a steel rule graduated over 300 mm in steps of 1 mm and numbered in centimetres, its zero at the end of the rule. It stands upright on a stand on the bench, behind the tail of the car.
- PH-29: a steel rule graduated over 150 mm in steps of 1 mm and numbered in centimetres, its zero at the end of the rule. It stands upright on the bench against the left side wall of the chassis tub, its zero end down.
- PH-30, PH-31, PH-32: the kitchen scale of BL-03. It gives a reading in grams; its own size is not calibrated.

Nothing else in the photographs has a calibrated size: no part of the chassis or of the deck, no hole or slot of
the deck plate, and nothing in the room behind the bench. The bought-in parts of BL-04 are the parts in the
photographs, and their data pages print their outlines.

**BL-09. An old drawing.** The lab's files hold one drawing of the deck plate, revision A, which gives the
plate a length of 310 mm. The plate on the car was cut to a later revision, of which no drawing was kept,
and its length is not that of revision A. Revision A is not a description of the car.
